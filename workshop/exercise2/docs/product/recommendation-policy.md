# Recommendation policy

This document defines the rules that govern how Northwind Energy recommends energy plans
to customers.

These rules must be followed whenever the product provides guidance toward a specific plan.

---

## Core rules

### 1. A recommendation must explain why it fits

A recommendation is not useful if it only names a plan. It must explain which aspect of the
customer's stated situation or preferences makes that plan a good fit.

**Example of a weak recommendation:**
> "We recommend Zeker."

**Example of a recommendation that meets this rule:**
> "We recommend Zeker because you told us predictable monthly costs matter to you. Zeker's
> fixed price means your bill stays the same throughout the year."

---

### 2. Show relevant trade-offs

Even when a plan is recommended, the customer must be shown what they are giving up. The
trade-off should relate to the plans that were not chosen, and should be honest.

**Example:**
> "The main trade-off is that if market prices fall significantly, you would not benefit —
> unlike on Flex, where the price follows the market."

---

### 3. No plan is objectively the best

The product must never describe one plan as objectively the best, the cheapest overall, the
most responsible choice, or similar absolute claims.

Each plan is better than the others on specific dimensions. A plan is a good recommendation
for a specific customer, not for all customers.

---

### 4. Do not recommend purely on lowest price

The cheapest estimated monthly cost is not a sufficient reason to recommend a plan.
The recommendation must be grounded in the customer's stated preferences, not in
optimising for price alone.

---

### 5. Use only information the customer has provided

The recommendation must be based on what the customer actually told the product. Do not
infer preferences the customer did not express, and do not make assumptions about their
situation beyond what was stated.

---

### 6. Customers can always compare all plans and choose differently

After a recommendation is shown, the customer must always be able to view all three plans
and select a different one. The product must not remove the comparison or make it difficult
to override the recommendation.

---

### 7. Do not force a recommendation when preferences conflict or are unclear

Some customers will give answers that point in different directions. For example, a customer
who values both price certainty and renewable sourcing but finds €142/month too high is in a
genuine conflict.

When preferences conflict or do not point clearly to one plan, the product should say so
honestly rather than forcing a recommendation. Showing relevant trade-offs and letting the
customer decide is the right outcome in these cases.

---

## What a good recommendation experience looks like

A customer who values predictable costs and does not mention environmental preferences
should receive a recommendation for Zeker with a clear explanation of why, a note about
the early exit fee, and an easy path to compare Groen and Flex if they wish.

A customer who explicitly cares about renewable sourcing should receive a recommendation
for Groen, with an honest note that Groen costs slightly more than Zeker.

A customer who says they move frequently and want to avoid a fixed commitment should
receive a recommendation for Flex, with a note that the variable price can be higher in
expensive market periods.

A customer who says they want the cheapest option should not receive a simple "Zeker is
cheapest" response. Instead, the product should explain that Zeker has the lowest estimated
monthly cost but the actual cost depends on usage, and that Flex could be cheaper or more
expensive depending on market conditions.
