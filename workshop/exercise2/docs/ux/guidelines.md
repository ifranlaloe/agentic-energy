# UX guidelines

These guidelines apply to any experience that helps customers choose an energy plan.

---

## Ask only for information that changes the outcome

Each question should affect which plan is recommended or what trade-offs are shown.
If removing a question would not change any recommendation or trade-off, do not ask it.

A short experience with two or three meaningful questions is better than a long form
with six questions that all lead to the same result.

---

## Use clear, plain-language choices

Avoid energy industry jargon. Customers are not required to understand market mechanisms,
contract law, or grid infrastructure.

If a concept needs explaining to be understood, that explanation should be part of the
choice, not something the customer must already know.

---

## Explain the recommendation

Do not show a recommendation without an explanation. The explanation should connect the
customer's answers to the recommended plan in plain language.

A customer who understands why they are being recommended something is better equipped
to evaluate whether they agree.

---

## Keep alternatives visible

After a recommendation, the customer must be able to see the other plans and choose one
of them instead. The product must not hide alternatives or make them difficult to reach.

Never present the comparison as "recommended vs. not recommended." All three plans are
valid choices; the recommendation reflects the customer's stated preferences, not an
objective ranking.

---

## Avoid pressure and urgency

Do not use language that pressures the customer to choose quickly, suggests this is
a limited-time opportunity, or implies they are making a mistake if they choose differently.

---

## Reuse existing interaction patterns

The application already has components for cards, buttons, and forms. A new experience
should feel like a natural part of the product, not a separate tool grafted on.

---

## No dead ends

Every path through the experience must have a clear way forward. If a recommendation
cannot be made, tell the customer clearly and offer them the comparison instead.

A customer who finishes the experience without a plan they feel good about is a
worse outcome than a customer who was shown the comparison and chose for themselves.

---

## Mobile-friendly by default

The existing application is responsive. Any new experience must work on a phone-width
screen without horizontal scrolling or overlapping elements.
